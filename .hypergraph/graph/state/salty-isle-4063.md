## Current

**A policy reads only what an onboard sensor measures (ADR-408, commit `c11ed048`) [rec: nimble-meadow-6874].** `api.sensor(target, kind, name=)` declares an `imu` on a component (grounds its orientation and rotation rate) or a `joint_encoder` on a joint (its position and rate); `api.observation` gains `role="policy"|"privileged"` and `sensor=`, and the API refuses a sensor passed to what it does not measure or is not mounted on. Task rows carry `role`/`grounded_sensor`/`grounded_kind` only when not the default, so older tasks export byte-identical and their policies still verify; policies are verified against `CadexDynamics.policy_channels`. Packaged gate after the merge: 23 passed. Motivated by hex2, whose policy read joint angles an MG90S cannot report and a CoM velocity nothing measures [rec: polished-path-3774].

**A weld written unconnected-first exported its body at the *inverse* of its pose, for as long as the exporter has existed — found, fixed and pinned (ADR-393, commit `63236e54`) [rec: pale-dew-4616] [rec: glad-chart-3979].** It surfaced on F7's biped, the first ot7 design with rotated components: composing `plover_model-model.xml`'s body tree to world against the solved placements, **all 24 of `ot7-plover-e`'s 29 components whose placement carries a non-identity rotation were at a different world pose in the model than in the assembly** — worst `c_tabscrew_knee_l_0` at 121.9 mm with a wholly different orientation — while the five with an identity rotation were exact to 1e-6. Sharper: of the 28 non-root bodies, **4 carried the parent-relative transform the solved assembly implies and 24 carried its exact inverse, none anything else** — the four are precisely the bodies with a revolute joint, the 24 are the fixed attachments [rec: pale-dew-4616].

*The cause was not where the first reading put it.* `CadexDynamics.py`'s `L_p ∘ inv(L_c)` derivation of a non-root body's frame is **correct and unchanged**: a fixture built forwards, with connector frames labelled by the components they belong to, recovers the solved parent-relative transform for a weld and a hinge alike. What was wrong was the labelling of its two inputs. `JointObject.setJointConnectors` calls `ensureUnconnectedIsSecondRef` (upstream FreeCAD issue 29355), which swaps `Reference1`/`Reference2` **together with** `Placement1`/`Placement2`, `Offset1`/`Offset2` and `Detach1`/`Detach2` whenever the first reference's part is the unconnected one; `cadex_assembly_worker._build_joint` read `Placement{i}` at the *script's* own connector index while naming the component from the script's own connector list, so after a swap each component was handed the other one's frame. Correct arithmetic on mislabelled inputs. The trigger is the ordinary weld — `joint("fixed", connector(part, "origin"), connector(host, …))` puts the unconnected part first — which is the 24-and-4 split exactly. `_native_connector_sides` now maps each script connector to the native slot whose reference names its component, and a joint whose native references cannot be matched one-to-one is **refused at `stage: native_connector_frames`** rather than published [rec: glad-chart-3979].

*Measured, then pinned, then its reach measured too.* A three-component live-`cadexd` assembly — a plate grounded at 37° about +Z, a tab welded unconnected-first, an arm hinged host-first — reproduced the inversion in one run; `test_dynamics_connector_sides_live.py` is that assembly, and both its tests fail on the old code (verified by reverting the worker). `pixi run test-engine` **2195 passed, 53 skipped**; packaged gate **23 passed**; the plover script rebuilt through a fixed engine has **all 29 bodies agreeing** with their solved placements [rec: glad-chart-3979]. The historical reach is **only assemblies with a weld written unconnected-first, and only through frames** — a swapped joint's published `local_frame`/`global_frame`, its MJCF body pose, and any swept-clearance row using them. Solved placements, clearance measurements and every static fit check read component placements and were never affected, which is why `ot7-plover-e` reported zero failing fit checks on a model whose bodies were inverted [rec: glad-chart-3979]. That claim was then **measured rather than argued** (ADR-394): `docs/probes/ot7/runner/mjcf_agreement.py` composes an exported body tree to world against the solve's `component_placements`, and on the three retained ot6 designs — Finch, Robin and Heron, whose `purchase()`, `fix_{name}` and `weld()` all pass the carrying component's connector first — **every body agrees at exactly 0.0 mm and 0.0° across all four retained attempts of each** (0 of 29, 0 of 24, 0 of 15), with each design's MJCF byte-identical across all four. `ot7-plover-e`'s pre-fix model is the probe's nonzero control at 24 of 29 [rec: weathered-pebble-8544].

*And verified end to end on the design that found it [rec: fresh-dawn-0892].* Re-exported through the fixed engine, `ot7-plover-e`'s model agrees with its own solve at **0 of 29 bodies disagreeing** — worst 8.620781476393591e-33 mm and 1.2074182697257333e-06° — against the accepted pin's 24 of 29 at 121.86102740417053 mm, and the rollout that model feeds passes **every** smoke check: finite state, 0 penetration breaches at 0.321 mm against a 0.5 mm tolerance, resting after a 0.268 mm drop at 0.182° of tilt, and the exact-BREP half clearing **406 of 406 pairs across all 51 samples** with `initial_pose_agrees: true` where the pre-fix model returned no verdict at all. The fix is therefore measured at both ends: zero historical reach on the three retained ot6 designs [rec: weathered-pebble-8544], and a passing rollout on the one design that carried the defect. Reopening that design to spend its remaining slots cost a separate engine change, because the better export failed the project digest (ADR-396, detail on `forest-wind-0342`) [rec: frosty-sea-6051].

**A free base is ordinary dynamics surface (ADR-335).** `extract_tree` accepts an ungrounded assembly and gives its first component a free joint through the island path. Only when nothing is grounded, `build_model` adds one infinite plane `environment/floor` on the world body at z = 0, using MuJoCo default contact parameters and recording them as `dynamics.environment.floor`; a grounded model gets no floor and `environment: null`. `test_dynamics_free_base.py` pins the floor, resting block and manifest; engine suite 2114 passed, 53 skipped and packaged gate 16 passed. `MUJOCO.md` §2 documents the surface; hazard 11 still applies to additional islands nothing holds [rec: loyal-canyon-4623].

Dynamics and control is ordinary product surface on the one branch, not a vertical on a fork [rec: open-key-6334]. It is operations on the existing `assembly` domain — **no sixth domain, no protocol op, no output type of its own** — which is why the whole arc cost so little contract [rec: sage-wood-0687].

- The arc runs end to end: `assembly.dynamics` simulates a solved assembly with exact OCCT inertias, `assembly.mjcf` exports one self-contained file that loads in a stock MuJoCo and verifies its own output before returning it, `assembly.task` states a control problem as data, `assembly.policy` verifies returned weights against a recorded witness, and `assembly.rollout` plays it back in the viewport [rec: sage-wood-0687].
- **Live mode**: a running mechanism the user can push with the mouse, at **344 µs a control step (29× real time)** and a 1.72 ms median round trip against a 33 ms bar, identical from the staged payload. `endless` plus `record_steps=False` makes a half-hour session cost +1.6 MB instead of +553 MB [rec: mellow-hawk-8610].
- MuJoCo is a **kernel we keep, not a tree we fork** — upstream, unmodified, and `mujoco == 3.10.0` exactly pinned, arriving as a pypi wheel carried by name because the conda manifest is not re-solvable against the `occt` pin [rec: sage-wood-0687].
- **Three invariants, all test-pinned and all cheap to break by accident**: nothing in `shell/` imports mujoco; `CadexDynamics.py` is reachable from the sandboxed worker and **never** from `cadexd`; and no `jax` or `mjx` anywhere under `src/Mod/cadex` or in a staged payload [rec: sage-wood-0687].
- Two observation kinds were added by the RL work and cost one table row each: `centre_of_mass_velocity` and `centroidal_angular_momentum` [rec: humble-path-4466].
- MJX and stock MuJoCo are the same physics to float64 machine epsilon with collision disabled and with a `plane` floor. They differ **only about box against box** — which is what `export_mjcf` writes for every grounded body [rec: humble-path-4466].
- **A published rollout trace is also a load-case source**, and this needed nothing new from the engine. `analysis/loads_from_rollout.py` replays a `cadex-assembly-simulation-trace-v1` in stock MuJoCo and reads `mj_rnePostConstraint`'s `cfrc_int` (the joint reaction wrench between a body and its parent) and `cfrc_ext` (contact and applied) — so `contact_force` being a deferred *engine* observation does not block structural work on mechanism parts, because this runs offboard [rec: fair-beacon-5964].

**The `assembly.mjcf` stall on a many-core host is fixed, and it was never a dynamics declaration (ADR-250, commit `2b881775`).** On the ot4 machine a walk-designed ten-component catalog rig hit four separate 300 s worker kills (returncode −24, SIGXCPU) on `assembly.mjcf` and `assembly.dynamics`, so the design agent gated its whole training layer off behind `policy_on=0` and the walk failed at `train` [rec: open-hollow-2140]. **The bisect cleared every declaration**: all ten bodies with no collision shapes at all still burned 299.1 s, and the assembly cut to two components and one revolute joint still burned 300.0 s. Profiling with py-spy `--subprocesses` (ptrace attach is blocked) put **119.4 s of 120 sampled in one import chain** — `mujoco/__init__.py` → `numpy/__init__.py` → the dlopen of `_multiarray_umath`. The cause is **OpenBLAS sizing a per-thread scratch pool from the host's core count**: address space reserved by `import mujoco, numpy` inside `FreeCADCmd` on this 32-core box is 216 MB at 1 thread, 352 at 2, 624 at 4, 1,168 at 8 and **4,432 MB unset**, against the worker's 6,144 MB `RLIMIT_AS`. The mapping is refused and OpenBLAS spins in its allocation retry loop until `RLIMIT_CPU` kills it; a stalled worker reads `VmSize: 6291360 kB` — the ceiling exactly — against `VmRSS: 169816 kB`. On a four-core laptop it fits, which is why nothing saw it until the loop moved to a build box. Fixed by pinning `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4` in the worker environment (the engine is not BLAS-bound; `analysis/` is offboard) — the walk's own script at `policy_on=1` goes **300.0 s / exit 3 → 2.0 s**, the two bisect variants 300.0 → 0.8 s and 299.1 → 1.2 s, and the **full dynamics layer** (ten bodies with per-component collisions, the servo actuator with real limits, joint dynamics, four observations, reward, termination, randomisation and both ranged `assembly.disturbance` values) **accepts in 1.2 s, where nothing of it had ever built**. Engine suite 2086 passed / 53 skipped. The caps themselves are unchanged by decision — raising them would have hidden this [rec: lawful-wolf-9205].

**The engine now refuses a task MJX cannot build, where it is written (ADR-281).** `CadexDynamics.candidate_collision_pairs` writes out MuJoCo's static contact filter — explicit `<pair>` rows, exclusion signatures, the same-weld and parent-child filters, the contype/conaffinity masks — because the engine may not import MJX (ADR-084), and `mjx_unsupported_collision_pairs` reports the survivors whose geom type pair MJX has no contact function for: **box/cylinder, cylinder/mesh, box/ellipsoid, ellipsoid/mesh**. `task_records` raises `mjx_unsupported_collision_pair` naming both geoms, both bodies and both kinds, at the moment the task is declared. Verified two ways against real MJX from the training venv: the hand-written filter reproduces `mjx.geom_pairs` exactly on the walk's own exported model (3 pairs each, identical sets), and the static table matches `mjx.has_collision_fn`. `assembly.mjcf`, `assembly.rollout` and the simulation trace are untouched, and the refusal costs no other surface because a task is only ever read by the offboard trainer. Engine suite 2,099 passed / 54 skipped; CLI 289 passed [rec: sleepy-grove-5790].

**These are the newest and least settled surfaces in the product.** The author rates the training and demonstration panels as "not fully fleshed out — most of what you need, but not quite" [rec: western-badger-3023].

## Negative knowledge

- [scope: a non-root MJCF body at the exact inverse of its pose | confidence: high | evidence: glad-chart-3979] The arithmetic is not the suspect. `L_p ∘ inv(L_c)` is right for a weld and a hinge alike; what inverts the result is the two frames arriving under each other's names, because FreeCAD's `ensureUnconnectedIsSecondRef` swaps `Placement1`/`Placement2` with `Reference1`/`Reference2` and a reader indexing by the *script's* connector order never sees it. A clean exoneration by a fixture at the layer you suspected is a result: move one layer down to where the inputs get their names.
- [scope: a design's fit report as evidence its exported model is right | confidence: high | evidence: pale-dew-4616, glad-chart-3979] They read different things. Static clearance, the sweep and the inventory all read component **placements**, which the connector swap never touched, so `ot7-plover-e` measured zero failing fit checks while 24 of its 29 bodies sat at the wrong world pose in the MJCF — four of them carrying collision geoms and all 24 carrying mass. A dynamics pass on such a model is a pass on geometry that does not match the design; only a frame-0 agreement check sees it.
- [scope: "no difference" as the answer to a regression question | confidence: high | evidence: weathered-pebble-8544] It is worthless unless it was looked for with a tool shown returning the right nonzero. `mjcf_agreement.py` was pointed at `ot7-plover-e`'s pre-fix model first — 24 of 29 disagreeing, worst 121.86102740417053 mm at 179.99999879258172°, iteration 167's figure recomputed by an independent route — and only then at the three designs where it returned zero.
- [scope: CadexDynamics imports | confidence: high | evidence: sage-wood-0687] mujoco and scipy.spatial must stay deferred imports inside functions. A service whose job is reading NDJSON off a pipe does not need 53.5 MB of physics engine resident, and test_engine_purity_guardrails asserts cadexd's import closure exactly.
- [scope: `import mujoco` inside the sandboxed worker on a many-core host | confidence: high | evidence: lawful-wolf-9205] numpy's OpenBLAS reserves a per-thread scratch buffer at dlopen and sizes the pool from the *host's* core count, so the worker's address-space footprint is a property of the machine rather than of the model — ~136 MB per thread, 4.4 GB unset on 32 cores. Under `RLIMIT_AS` that is not an out-of-memory error: the mapping is refused and OpenBLAS retries until `RLIMIT_CPU` kills it, so a pure-import stall presents as a compute timeout on whatever op happened to import first. Pinned to four threads for the same reason `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` is pinned — a worker that behaves differently on a laptop and on a build box is the bug, not a speed knob.
- [scope: a worker kill on a long dynamics op | confidence: high | evidence: open-hollow-2140, lawful-wolf-9205] The sandbox cap is an `RLIMIT_CPU` in **CPU-seconds** (`cadex_domain_worker.py:_resource_limits`, `DEFAULT_SCRIPTED_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 300.0`) while the parent's timeout is the same number in **wall-clock** seconds, so a threaded pass on a many-core box reaches the CPU limit first. Since ADR-250 that kill is legible — SIGXCPU and SIGXFSZ surface as `DOMAIN_CPU_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` / `DOMAIN_OUTPUT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` naming the cap and the unit asymmetry, not as the opaque `The isolated domain worker exited without a result`. Script-level `print(..., flush=True)` is still lost on the kill, so instrumenting from inside the script cannot locate a stall: time whole runs from outside, and if the bisect clears every declaration, profile the import rather than the model.
- [scope: MuJoCo defaults | confidence: high | evidence: sage-wood-0687] A default is a promise, not a decision. The translator pins its simulation options. ADR-335 explicitly uses MuJoCo default contact parameters for the environment floor and records them in the manifest [rec: loyal-canyon-4623].
- [scope: reading a wrench out of MuJoCo | confidence: high | evidence: fair-beacon-5964] `cfrc_int` and `cfrc_ext` are **com-based**: the torque is about `subtree_com[body_rootid[body]]`, not about the body. Read without moving it onto the body (`t_p = t_c + (c - p) x F`), the forces still check out and the moments are wrong by `r x F` — which on a leg is the whole number. The failure is silent in exactly the way that survives a review.
- [scope: replaying a trace to measure anything | confidence: high | evidence: fair-beacon-5964] A replay is only the rollout if it **tracked** the rollout, so check it frame by frame against the trace's own recorded poses rather than assuming. Author a rollout at `frames_per_second` equal to the control rate when you intend to read loads off it: a trace sampled more coarsely holds only some of the actions, and measured on a two-link leg the same motion recorded half as often replayed **142 mm** away from itself where an exact one replays to 0.0 mm. The trace's frame convention is the other trap — an untimed `input` frame, then an **unstepped** `solver_output` at t=0 carrying no commands, then one frame per action.
- [scope: a design turn's own account of why a dynamics op refused | confidence: high | evidence: open-hollow-2140, lawful-wolf-9205] Do not take it as a measurement. The ot4 rig's `docs/rejected.md` asserted that a single grounded box with one box collision exhausts the worker cap; a control built that model in under a second, and the real cause was in neither the model nor the export.
- [scope: trajectory comparison on a contacting biped | confidence: high | evidence: humble-path-4466] Trajectory-level agreement between MJX and stock MuJoCo can never be had — a 1e-7 nudge inside stock MuJoCo alone separates the trajectory just as fast. The two are comparable statistically and in no other way.

- [scope: declaring a shape collides with nothing, as an escape from a contact filter | confidence: high | evidence: sleepy-grove-5790] `collides_with=[]` does not make a decorative geom contact-free. MuJoCo's mask test is an `or` over both directions, so a shape declaring it collides with nothing is still touchable by a shape that collides with everything — the module's own `contact_masks` docstring says so, and the first draft of ADR-281's correction text offered the wrong escape until its own test failed on it. Both sides must agree.
- [scope: an MJX `NotImplementedError` on a geom type pair | confidence: high | evidence: sleepy-grove-5790] It is a real MJX kernel limit, not an exporter defect. The `ot4-mix52` script authored `assembly.collision("cylinder", …)` for a guide rail, the exporter emitted exactly that, stock MuJoCo simulates it against a box, and every engine check passed. The fix that helps is moving *when the author finds out*, not changing what the exporter emits.
