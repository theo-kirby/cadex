This project is the copy of ot10's accepted quadruped that you have already trained four times this week to walk with real steps at a commanded speed. This is a second session of the same loop. The behaviour, the fixed mechanism, the frozen success spec and the rules for training are unchanged from the first session: the spec is in the script as `walk_spec`, passed to the task as `success=walk_spec` with `goals=[command]`, and you must never loosen, drop or reword a predicate, a seed, a condition or the goal. Do not change the geometry, the bodies or their collision shapes, the joints or their limits, or the actuators, and do not change the parameters that set them. Observations, sensors and everything about the task are yours.

WHERE THE LOOP STANDS. Call train_status with no run first and read every run and evaluation. All four runs, r1-clearance, r2-bounded, r3-nochatter and r4-anglesonly, failed all ten seeds.

A FACT YOU DID NOT HAVE IN THE FIRST SESSION (ADR-465). Until 23:11Z on 2026-09-30 the trainer integrated a servo whose torque sat at its limit with extra damping that the engine does not apply. MJX 3.10 folds the actuator's velocity gain into its implicit step even when the force is clamped; stock MuJoCo, which `evaluate` runs, leaves it out. Every servo on this robot is exported that way, and a 9 g servo is at its limit most of the time. So r1-clearance, r2-bounded and r3-nochatter trained on physics the evaluator does not run. Their reward curves are not evidence about their reward designs: r2's policy scored +2.48 per step in training and walked forwards there, and the engine walked the same weights backwards. The trainer has been fixed, and the same policy now walks backwards in the trainer too. Their evaluations stand, because the engine measured them. The gap between training and evaluation reward that you diagnosed in rounds 3 and 4 as closed-loop instability was largely this. r4-anglesonly is the only run trained on the fixed trainer, so its evaluation is the first whose training curve and evaluation describe the same machine. Read its evaluation (the rows, the metrics, the per-foot numbers, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips) and compare its training reward with its evaluation reward before deciding anything. r4-anglesonly changed two things at once, the policy's inputs and the trainer it ran on, so its closed gap cannot be credited to the inputs alone. Changes you made to answer the old gap are yours to keep or undo.

THE RULES FOR TRAINING. Unchanged from the first session. Every run is pre-registered by train_start with a wall-clock budget of at most 2400 seconds; for scale, 1000 iterations of 2048 environments took about 1900 s on this machine. One run at a time. The training seed is never one of 1101-1110. A run's reason cites a measurement from the previous evaluation: the failing predicate, its measured value and the limit. After each run: store the policy, declare it on the task it was trained on, call evaluate, and read the verdict, the per-seed rows, the metrics, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips. A failed evaluation is diagnosed before the next run: which predicate failed, by how much, on which seeds, and what in the task you believe caused it; then say whether the change helped.

WHEN TO STOP. This session is at most three runs. Stop when an evaluation passes on all ten seeds, or when seven runs have been trained and evaluated in total on this project (four from the first session and three from this one), whichever comes first. Do not stop after a failed evaluation while runs remain. End with a short report: each run of this session, what it changed and why, its evaluation, and what you would change next.
