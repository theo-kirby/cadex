## What this taught

**Freezing the evaluation before training is what made the results
believable.** P1 made ot10's shuffle and ot9's wandering Robin fail for the
right reasons before any new training. Every confirmation was
pre-registered and run once. Balance, reach and walk all reached 10 of 10
plus the blind judge's bar, and the owner's review of the videos agreed.

**The still-frame judge cannot see slip or stepping.** Two probes showed
it (ADR-460, ADR-461). The owner's rule settled it mid-run: the spec is
authoritative for anything it measures, and the judge covers what the spec
cannot. Write that division into future charters from the start.

**Walking was mostly blocked by simulation defects, not reward design.**
- The trainer's servo physics disagreed with the engine's (ADR-465).
- The floor contact was soft enough for feet to sink 7–15 mm (ADR-469).
- The agent tried a contact margin to pass the floor check. The owner's
  rule turned that into a product refusal (ADR-470).

Walk went from 0 of 10 on every round to 10 of 10 within about ten rounds
of the contact fix. When a learned behaviour stalls, check sim parity and
contact physics before blaming the reward.

**The loop detector's no-progress escalation is noise in a
report_done charter whose criteria close late.** It reached level 3
while walk climbed 0→1→2→9→10 of 10. `rotate: false` kept it harmless.
