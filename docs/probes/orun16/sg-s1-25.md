## The judge: `sg_judge.py`

`orun11/judge.py` for `gallop_sg`: it checks the policy against
`gallop_sg-task.json` and its model as judge.py does, asserts the spec is
`sg_spec` with one held `cmd` segment, and for each `--cmd` writes the
judged copy of the bundle with `success.goal[cmd].sequence = [cmd]`, then
`evaluations/OUT_NAME-<cmd>/` with judge.py's `closure` block and
`judged_command_mm_s`. The spec is outside the task's semantic digest, so
the swap leaves the trained task alone.

**Checked first** on 25 (`sgcheck`, since deleted), commands 1000 and 2400:
each seed's `drawn.goal` is one segment 0–10 s holding exactly the
command (`values: [1000.0]`, `[2400.0]`), so the command reaches the
engine. The check also showed the evaluation plays the *spec's* seeds,
not the inputs' list, so `--seeds` now rewrites the judged spec's seeds.

**Read.** The policy at 25 has learnt to stay up and to step at the
reference's rate, which rises with the command (touchdowns 2.0 → 3.1 Hz
against the strides' 2.0 → 3.0), but it makes no ground: it marches in
place, drifting back at the higher commands. With no stride, no pair lag
is measured (the engine's `pair_lag_*_median` is `None` on most seeds),
there is no flight, and the heel share and U say nothing yet about a
schedule: the heel share is flat or falling with the command, and U and
LH's toes-up lift rise with it, the touch-down fold of a paw stamping in
place. The hips, hocks, spine and tail sit at their clamp on nearly every
seed (a cold, high-σ policy, σ 0.81 at 26).

**The rule at 25** (`sg-task.md`, written before judging): stop only on a
blow-up or a policy that falls on most seeds at every command. Neither:
no blow-up (loops ≤ 1.8 mm, pins ≤ 920 °/s max), and 0–2 of 10 tipped.
**sg-s1 runs on to 50.** The trainer's curve is climbing: reward per step
12.0 at 10, 15.2 at 24, 16.3 at 27, episode return 119 → 2,100 over
13–27. The rule at 50 stands as written: stop and change one lever if
the 2400 command's heel share is not above the 1000 command's on either
leg, or the 2400 command runs under 1.9 m/s.
