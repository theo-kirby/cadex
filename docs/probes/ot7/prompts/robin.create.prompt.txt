Create a NEW parametric two-wheeled balancing robot called Robin in this empty project. This is a fresh design: do not import, link, copy or consult anything from any other project, mechanism, checkpoint, policy or earlier conversation. Do not train anything in this turn. Complete this single design turn without asking questions.

The bar this design is measured against: it must be a machine somebody could build. Every purchased part comes from the catalog (`lib.*`; inspect the catalog families and specs first and use their datasheet numbers as datums), every other solid is a modelled printable part with a named purpose, and no solid is a bare box or cylinder standing in for something. Nothing in the world is part of the design: no floor, slab, wall or stage. The floor is the environment's plane at z = 0 (world +X forward, +Y left, +Z up); the robot stands on it with its wheel treads tangent to z = 0.

The machine, from the catalog:
- Two `lib.gearmotor("pololu-2367")` N20 gearmotors, one per side, shafts coaxial on the lateral (world Y) axis, shaft tips pointing outboard. Their stall torque and no-load speed come from the spec, not from a guess.
- Two modelled printable wheels (about 60 mm diameter, about 8 mm wide tread), each with a hub whose D-bore matches the catalog shaft's diameter and flat (declared bore clearance, e.g. 0.1 mm), pressed onto the shaft so the hub face clears the gearbox front face and its boss by a declared gap. The wheel is one printed solid: hub, disc or spokes, rim.
- One modelled printable chassis: a body that carries the two motors, the board and the battery volume. Each motor sits in a pocket sized from the spec's width/height/rear envelope with a declared clearance per side (e.g. 0.3 mm), located by the gearbox front face against a printed shoulder, and is retained by a modelled printed clamp bar held by two catalog M2 socket screws (`lib.bolt`) into catalog M2 heat-set inserts (`lib.heat_set_insert`) in the chassis. The chassis mounts a `lib.board("pi-zero-2-w")` flat on top on four catalog M2 socket screws through the board's own mount holes into four M2 heat-set inserts in printed bosses; put the board's mount-hole positions from its spec, not from memory. The chassis has an open battery bay: a pocket declared by parameters for a 2S pack of two 18650 cells (about 38 x 66 x 20 mm plus a declared clearance), low in the body between the motors. Do not model the cells as a solid; state the bay size and the pack mass it is sized for in the design specs and say plainly that the pack's mass is not in the model. The chassis may be tall (the board and bay above the axle) so the centre of mass sits above the axle, as a balancing robot's must.
- Use `lib.*` parts only for what the catalog has; the wheels, chassis and clamp bars are printed.

Declare parameters with sensible defaults in millimetres and kg/m3 and use them everywhere: wheel_dia, wheel_w, hub_len, track (wheel centre to wheel centre), motor_clear, bore_clear, hub_gap, chassis_w, chassis_d, chassis_h, wall_t, bay_w, bay_d, bay_h, bay_clear, printed_density (PLA ~1240), plus the task and lifecycle parameters below. Also declare a numeric parameter policy_on with default 0, following the project's iterate convention, so that a trained policy can later be switched on by `cadex walk` without editing geometry; while policy_on is 0 nothing about the model changes.

Dynamics, declared in the script for MuJoCo, following the project's own guidance:
- The chassis is the free base: declare it as the FIRST component in script order and first in the assembly's component list, ground nothing, and give it no fixed joint to the world. Every purchased part and clamp is fixed to the component that carries it with a `fixed` joint; each wheel turns on a `revolute` joint about the lateral axis with NO angle limits (continuous rotation), with both connectors of every joint given the identical posed world frame so the assembly solves with zero residual at the upright pose.
- Masses from geometry: `assembly.body` for every solid, printed parts at printed_density, catalog parts at the density their spec gives (steel 7850 for screws and inserts, the board's own spec density, the gearmotor's effective density from its spec mass).
- Collision proxies, declared per body, never the tessellation: boxes for the chassis (one per wall or bay region is fine), the motors, the clamps and the board; each wheel a SPHERE of the wheel's outer radius at the wheel centre (the tread's rolling contact), with a declared friction; screws and inserts carry mass only. Record in a comment how each proxy relates to its solid. At t = 0 only the two wheels touch the floor and no two proxies of the design touch each other.
- Actuators: one `assembly.actuator(kind="motor", control_nmm="0", torque_limit_nmm=<the gearmotor spec's stall torque>)` per wheel joint, and `assembly.joint_dynamics` on each wheel joint with damping derived from the spec's stall torque and no-load speed, plus a small armature.
- Observations: chassis orientation, chassis angular velocity, chassis position, wheel joint velocities, centre of mass and its velocity, and each actuator's force.
- A balance task (`assembly.task`): reward an alive bonus, a penalty proportional to the chassis's pitch angle (its orientation about the lateral axis) and a small control cost; end the episode when the chassis has fallen, declared as its frame height dropping below a declared fraction of its upright height (a parameter fall_frac, default 0.7) — say in a comment which channel the termination reads. Episode length episode_steps (default 400) at control_hz (default 50), rollout_seed for the declared seed set 0-9, and a reset variation on the chassis of a small rigid tilt (0-3 degrees) with a small lift so the task cannot be won by bracing.
- Export the assembly and the MJCF/training task as publishable outputs so that `cadex train` finds exactly one training task; publish every solid as an output too, named so the wheel, motor, clamp, screw, insert, board and chassis are recognisable by name (e.g. wheel_l, motor_r, clamp_l, screw_clamp_l_0, insert_board_2).

Verify before accepting, through inspect facts and the script's own stdout: the solver reports solved with zero residual; every motor sits in its pocket with the declared clearance (pair distances between motor and chassis equal motor_clear and the common volume is 0); each wheel hub clears the gearbox front face by hub_gap; the wheel-to-shaft bore clearance is bore_clear; the board sits on its bosses at distance 0 with common volume 0; each screw's only intersection is its thread engagement in an insert or the clamp's clearance hole (state the expected engagement volume analytically); there are no other intersections and no unmeasured pairs; nothing is grounded and the dynamics export reports the chassis as the free base with the environment's floor.
