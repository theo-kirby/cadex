## Negative knowledge

- [scope: what the walk commits from a training leg | confidence: medium | evidence: shy-cabin-0798] Before ADR-199 the `train` leg's commit carried `job.cxpolicy`, `job.best.cxpolicy` and the store copy — three copies of one policy. The store's asset is the project (ADR-194); checkpoints and traces are not, and the `.gitignore` says so. Reversible per project, because the file is editable.
- [scope: `declare_policy` in `cli/cadex_cli/walk.py` against agent-authored scripts | confidence: high | evidence: misty-rain-9048, cool-fountain-2483] The rewrite handles inline `weights="…"` / `sha256="…"` literals only; a script that names them through module constants is refused at exit 3. The fix went into the authoring contract, not the rewrite: resolving constants would make the walk guess at a script it did not write. A test pins the refusal as intended behaviour.
- [scope: the first design-leg failure inside `cadex walk --prompt` on 2026-09-07 | confidence: medium | evidence: misty-rain-9048, ancient-wind-0117] One observed fast failure (3.76 s, after one `describe_api` call) with the child's reason discarded, and one identical retry that succeeded in 214 s. Its cause is unrecoverable: the envelope that would have named it landed afterwards. Not reproduced on purpose; the clean runs since [rec: placid-sky-7374] make it the outlier, not the pattern.
- [scope: what one prompt walk proves about a review surface | confidence: high | evidence: morning-summit-7848] The design turn is nondeterministic across runs of the same prompt: the pan-tilt prompt placed two catalog servos once and fused them into hand-authored solids the next time. A walk selected to exercise a catalog-part code path may not exercise it at all; check the inventory's catalogued count before reading its review numbers as evidence for anything catalog-shaped.
- [scope: comparing `total_reward` across prompt-walk projects | confidence: high | evidence: placid-sky-7374, empty-banner-7438, fond-star-1809] The reward expressions are different objectives in different units, so 425.997, 0.0035556 and -2380.357 compare runs of one project only, never designs against each other — the same caveat `examples/lifecycle/README.md` records for the two example mechanisms. The two leg walks make the same point on one mechanism: −5144.79 and 0.853 are the same leg under a negatively and a positively shaped objective.
- [scope: a walk project under the system temporary directory | confidence: high | evidence: lucid-pebble-4000, fond-star-1809] It is reaped between iterations, so its review findings cannot be written back into its own `DECISIONS.md`, and there is no conversation left to `--resume`. The findings survived only in this graph. An iterate unit must walk a durable project outside the checkout and outside `~/arch`.
- [scope: a design turn's own account of why an engine op refused | confidence: high | evidence: open-hollow-2140] Do not take it as a measurement. The ot4 rig's `docs/rejected.md` asserts that a single grounded box with one box collision exhausts the worker cap; a control in a fresh project builds that model in under a second. The turn writes what it inferred from the failure it saw, not what it isolated.
- [scope: walk evidence and the machine it ran on | confidence: high | evidence: open-hollow-2140, humble-forest-6896, lawful-wolf-9205] Six clean walks on one machine did not carry to the next one, and neither cause was in the walk. The design leg's model default was machine-specific (credit on one login, not another), and the dynamics stall was a native library sizing its allocations from the host's core count — so it needed a 32-core box to appear at all. "Tested headlessly" is a per-machine claim as long as the criterion words it that way, and the two machine-portability faults it caught were both worth catching.
- [scope: a design leg that fails in seconds carrying a provider's own sentence | confidence: high | evidence: dry-rain-0489, rising-rain-0117] It is a refusal, not a walk defect, and the walk already reports it correctly — exit 1 on leg `design`, the sentence verbatim in `walk.legs[0].error`, no later leg run, `review` empty. **Correction (2026-09-09):** "You're out of usage credits" was read here as an account-level block and it was not. The refusal was scoped to the walk's default model `claude-fable-5`; three other named models answered on the same login, and the very next walk under `CADEX_MODEL=claude-opus-5` ran a real design turn [rec: rising-rain-0117]. Probe the models before parking work on a credit premise — the shape of this was already in `open-hollow-2140` and was read as historical when it was current. The observed refusal re-staged `script.json` and overwrote the recorded model. **The model overwrite is repaired by ADR-276:** a failed same-locator turn now preserves the entire agent record; a new locator still persists with its attempted model. This does not claim to eliminate script restore bookkeeping [rec: restless-wave-7376].
- [scope: a design turn that ends at exit 3 with nothing refused and nothing offered | confidence: high | evidence: sage-glacier-2165, rising-rain-0117] It is not always the prompt. Three walks in one evening ended this way, and the last was diagnosed from the envelope alone: `stop_reason: end_turn`, 6,228 output tokens of which 5,893 were thinking and ~335 prose, the model reasoning to "*Finding 1 I believe and am taking*" and then ending the turn, project byte-for-byte unchanged. ADR-247 made that legible; legibility is not recovery, and an unwatched loop gets one design turn per invocation, so the walk now asks once more (ADR-274) — an offline-tested seam whose live path has never fired, because the first turn calls tools. **But check the prompt first**: the attempt before it exited 3 because the prompt named a part the project does not have and the turn correctly refused to invent one — a walk whose prompt names a part the project lacks is not evidence about the walk, and the next attempt's prompt should be read off the project's own `PROGRESS.md` [rec: rising-rain-0117].
- [scope: a token-free `cadex walk` as evidence | confidence: high | evidence: copper-canyon-0231] A walk with no `--prompt` runs every leg and all four review calls for ~24 s and no tokens, which makes it the cheap way to re-exercise the eyes on a real mechanism — but it runs **no design turn**, so it is evidence about the review step and the leg plumbing and none at all about the design leg, the provider, or learned control. Its reward row is the reference's, unchanged by construction; reporting one as a new seed would be a false measurement.
- [scope: what the walk's documentation eye proves | confidence: high | evidence: eager-lake-5745, western-gate-9567] It reads the exported MJCF's declared sections, so it can only ask for notes a mechanism declares — an undeclared subject is invisible to it, and a note that exists is counted, never read for content. On a **recipe** walk it will report every declared subject missing, and that is correct rather than a defect: `script --set` installs the recipe alone and no design turn runs, so there is nothing to have written the note [rec: western-gate-9567]. **Correction (2026-09-08):** this entry previously read "no walk on this machine has yet produced a domain note from a real design turn's closing text", and that was false — `docs/actuators.md` and `docs/sensors.md` in each of `ot4-carriage`, `ot4-swing2` and `ot4-swing` were committed by a `cadex prompt:` commit, so ADR-245's write half is exercised here three times over. Only the ADR-256 read-back eye had never run on a real prompt walk [rec: sleepy-hollow-9498].
- [scope: reading a `PROGRESS.md` travel or reward delta | confidence: high | evidence: northern-comet-5917, chilly-crest-2100] The delta is computed against the previous row **as written** — four significant figures — not against the raw float, because the row is what a later run can read; the raw figure stays in `review.json`. And a project's **first** walk carries no delta on any channel by construction, so an absent delta on a fresh project is not evidence that ADR-260's threading failed: `ot4-quill`'s baseline walk reported none, correctly, and the threading is evidenced by the offline regressions instead.
- [scope: a zero in one of the two travel channels | confidence: high | evidence: chilly-crest-2100] It is a fact about that rollout, not a missing measurement. The quill lift's cylindrical joint offers both a slide and a hinge and the rollout used only the slide — `travel_mm 20.28`, `travel_deg 0` — because gravity exerts no torque about a vertical axis, so an axially offset head mass does not spin the quill. Read a zero channel against the physics before reading it as a defect in the eye.
- [scope: bounding a walk leg by signalling it | confidence: high | evidence: tidy-cove-8382, honest-river-1137] Signal the **group**, kill it **unconditionally**, and capture the pgid while the child is alive. Every leg spawns something the walk does not control (`agent.py` uses `Popen` with no timeout of its own), so a direct-child kill leaves the process that was actually hanging alive, still holding the inherited stdout — and any drain after the stop must itself be bounded or it blocks on that pipe forever. Waiting on the direct child to decide whether to escalate is not a grace period: a polite child dies at once and the wait succeeds, which is exactly the case the bound exists for. A bound that only *looks* like it holds is worse than none, because the run reads as protected.
- [scope: reading a `review/section/.../XZ-3.125/` path as the walk's current shape | confidence: high | evidence: western-cliff-6876] Since ADR-267 the walk's section artifact directory is `XZ-<derived-offset>`, and the offset differs per mechanism (quill −21.2 mm, swing-arm rig −9.2 mm, carriage 0 mm). The committed `docs/probes/` baselines naming `XZ-3.125` are historical measurements and were deliberately left as written, so a `XZ-3.125` path in this repository dates a run rather than describing the leg.
- [scope: running the walk after a Python-only engine change | confidence: high | evidence: narrow-wing-0418, chilly-basin-7378] The installed runtime remains a separate copy requiring rebuild/install after source edits — `ot4-mix55` needed `pixi run build-engine` first, because the checkout's `CadexDynamics.py` differed from the install by exactly the previous iteration's commit. ADR-251 reports top-level Python differences or unavailable evidence before the walk; a `match` proves neither binary currency nor loaded-module provenance, and reporting never rebuilds or refuses.
- [scope: collision geometry a design turn may author for an MJX-trained walk | confidence: high | evidence: golden-dune-8756, sleepy-grove-5790, chilly-basin-7378] A **cylinder against a box** is valid MuJoCo and untrainable under MJX (`mjx.put_model` raises `NotImplementedError`), and it used to surface two seconds into the train leg — after a design leg that had already spent nineteen minutes and passed every engine, solver and MJCF check. **The cost asymmetry was the whole problem, and ADR-281 removed it**: `assembly.task` now refuses the four unsupported pairs at declaration time, naming both geoms, both bodies and both kinds [rec: sleepy-grove-5790]. Prefer box, capsule or sphere collision geometry; treat a cylinder as legal MuJoCo that may be an untrainable MJX geom. **Do not reach for `collides_with=[]`** as the escape for a decorative shape — MuJoCo's mask test is an `or` over both directions, so both sides must agree [rec: sleepy-grove-5790]. One unaided design turn has since authored only supported shapes without the refusal firing, which is evidence and not a guarantee [rec: chilly-basin-7378].
- [scope: reading a failed train leg's `error` out of `walk.json` | confidence: high | evidence: golden-dune-8756] Before ADR-280 it carried the trainer's **stdout** tail and pointed at a stderr no `--json` caller ever had, so an MJX `NotImplementedError` presented as two benign `Failed to import warp` warnings. Repaired — but the shape generalises: an envelope that names the wrong cause is worse than one that names none, because it sends the reader to the wrong half of the pipeline. Read a failure's exit code and duration against the named cause before believing it.
- [scope: a clearance intersection reported by a walk that exited 0 | confidence: high | evidence: chilly-basin-7378] Exit 0 does not mean no interference. `ot4-mix55` reported 648.0 mm³ of shared volume between frame and slider at the initial solved pose and still exited 0 — the designed behaviour under ADR-238, where clearance names the offending pair for the next design turn rather than gating the run. `docs/CLI.md` now says so explicitly rather than leaving a reader to infer the opposite.
