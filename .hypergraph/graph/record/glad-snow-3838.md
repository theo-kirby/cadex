## Why

The ladder says: run the walk, then close the named legs one unit each,
before anything else. Iteration 1 did the run [rec: misty-rain-9048]. It
completed the loop on both example recipes with numbers and landed
`review.json`, but it did not complete from a prompt, and the reconciled
state node now says why [rec: misty-rain-9048]: the design turn's authoring
contract in `cli/cadex_cli/agent.py` never says that `weights=` and
`sha256=` must be inline literals, so the model factored them into module
constants and `walk.py:declare_policy` refused at exit 3; and the design leg
failed once in 3.76 s with the child agent's reason discarded, then a
byte-identical retry succeeded in 214 s. Those are the two legs that still
need a person, and the criterion `crisp-reef-5607` stays open on exactly
them.

The mission-6 units in short (`flat-river-8853`) were promoted before nt3's
operator directive [rec: modest-summit-8554] made the walk lead until it is
closed. They are good units and they keep their specifications, but they
are not what the walk run named, so they go to medium ranks 1 and 2 and come
back the moment the walk from a prompt is closed or blocked.

Budget: one iteration ran at $3.35 and about twenty minutes, with 14.7 h of
run left. Three short units plus the re-run fit many times over; there is no
reason to promote medium work early.

Choice of first unit: the record recommended the smaller, reversible fix,
teaching the contract rather than widening `declare_policy` to resolve
module constants, which would make the walk guess at a script it did not
write [rec: misty-rain-9048]. That holds. The second unit is the failure
envelope, because an unattended retry policy cannot be written until the
walk carries the child's reason; the retry policy itself is a later unit.
The third unit is the re-run from `--prompt`, which the ladder asks for
whenever short empties and which is the only evidence that can tick the
criterion.
