This project is the copy of ot10's accepted quadruped that you have trained ten times, over three sessions, to walk with real steps at a commanded speed. This is a fourth session of the same loop. The behaviour, the frozen success spec, the evaluation seeds and the rules for training are unchanged. The spec is in the script as `walk_spec`, passed to the task as `success=walk_spec` with `goals=[command]`, and you must never loosen, drop or reword a predicate, a seed, a condition or the goal.

THE MECHANISM RULE IS UNCHANGED FROM SESSION 3. You may change the geometry, the bodies and their collision shapes and contact settings, the joints and their limits, the actuators, and the parameters that set them, through the ordinary edit and accept path, with these bounds:
- it stays a four-legged robot on one free base, and the spec's feet stay the four foot bodies `result["c_foot_fl"]`, `result["c_foot_fr"]`, `result["c_foot_rl"]` and `result["c_foot_rr"]`;
- the floor and its collision plane are not yours to change, and neither is anything that sets the physics for every body (timestep, gravity, solver);
- an actuator stays one the catalog offers, at its catalog limits.
The spec's thresholds are written in the mechanism's own scale, so three constants follow the mechanism and nothing else: `HIP_MM` and `WEIGHT_N` must equal the accepted model's hip height and weight, which the accepted attempt's task bundle states under `success.scale` (`hip_height_mm`, `weight_n`) and `evaluate` reports under `rig`; and `SPEC_LIFT` keeps its written formula. If a mechanism change moves either value, set the constant to the new value before you train. An evaluation whose `HIP_MM` or `WEIGHT_N` is not its model's is void. A run's reason says what changed and which measurement motivated it. The trainer refuses a warm start across a change to the model or to what the network reads or emits, so after such a change start fresh.

WHERE THE LOOP STANDS. Call train_status with no run first and read every run and evaluation. All ten evaluated runs failed all ten seeds. In session 3 the steel-ball feet took W10 (the deepest foot after the settle) to -0.021 to -0.043 hip heights on every seed of both rounds that used them, against the -0.05 limit, and no round of session 3 took a step: in all three the worst foot took 0 steps, W8-low was 0.00 and W3 was at most 0.15. In r10-steelfoot-fresh the policy stood still on three feet with the rear-right foot held up on all ten seeds; standing netted about +2.2 per step, mostly `alive`. In r11-speedpay the rear-right foot was again held up on all ten seeds, the mean forward speed was 1.4 to 2.1 mm/s against commands of 61 to 93 mm/s, and `speed_track` paid +1.0 to +1.65 per step against +0.25 for a body at rest: the tray's forward velocity, divided by the command, swung with a standard deviation of 0.47 to 0.84 around a median near zero. Your own closing report of session 3 diagnosed this and recorded what you would change next; it is in the project's DECISIONS.md. Read the evaluations themselves (the rows, the metrics, the per-foot numbers, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips) before deciding anything; these sentences are a summary, not the evidence.

THE RULES FOR TRAINING. Unchanged. Every run is pre-registered by train_start with a wall-clock budget of at most 2400 seconds; for scale, 800 iterations of 2048 environments took about 2,100 s on this machine. One run at a time. The training seed is never one of 1101-1110. A run's reason cites a measurement from the previous evaluation: the failing predicate, its measured value and the limit. After each run: store the policy, declare it on the task it was trained on, call evaluate, and read the verdict, the per-seed rows, the metrics, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips. A failed evaluation is diagnosed before the next run: which predicate failed, by how much, on which seeds, and what in the task or the mechanism you believe caused it; then say whether the change helped.

WHEN TO STOP. This session is at most three runs. Stop when an evaluation passes on all ten seeds, or when this session has trained and evaluated three runs, whichever comes first. A start the trainer refuses before its first iteration is not a run. Do not stop after a failed evaluation while runs remain. End with a short report: each run of this session, what it changed in the task or the mechanism and why, its evaluation, and what you would change next.
