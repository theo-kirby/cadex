This project is the copy of ot10's accepted quadruped that you have trained thirteen times, over four sessions, to walk with real steps at a commanded speed. This is a fifth session of the same loop. The behaviour, the frozen success spec, the evaluation seeds and the rules for training are unchanged. The spec is in the script as `walk_spec`, passed to the task as `success=walk_spec` with `goals=[command]`, and you must never loosen, drop or reword a predicate, a seed, a condition or the goal.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE PRODUCT SINCE SESSION 4. Two product decisions, neither of them yours, and one consequence for this project.
- The exported contact spring is stiffer (ADR-469). Every contact that does not bounce, and the floor the world carries, is now on a 0.004 s contact time constant instead of MuJoCo's default 0.02 s. A soft contact sinks in proportion to the acceleration pressing on it, and a servo driving a steel foot is a large acceleration: replaying r12-convex-sym's own policy on its own model over seeds 1101-1110, the deepest foot point after the settle was -6.18 to -7.62 mm on the old spring and -0.66 to -2.50 mm on the new one. Every policy trained so far trained on the old spring. A model accepted now carries the new one, so no earlier policy verifies against it and the trainer will refuse a warm start from any of them: start fresh.
- A contact margin or gap now voids an evaluation (the owner's rule for this criterion, applied by the product since ADR-470). A `margin`, a `gap` or any other setting that holds a contact surface off its geometry is not a fix for feet in the floor, whoever authors it: `evaluate` marks every seed of such a model void and names the geoms under `contact_offsets`. r13-hovercost-margin (3 mm) and r14-margin15-lift (1.5 mm) are both void for this reason.
- To rebuild the project on the new spring, the operator set `foot_margin` to 0 and `policy_on` to 0 and accepted the script again on the current engine. Nothing else in the script was changed: the task, the reward, the observations, the spec, the seeds and the goal are the ones you left at the end of session 4. The accepted model carries no contact offset. Check `HIP_MM` and `WEIGHT_N` against the accepted task bundle's `success.scale` before you train.

THE MECHANISM RULE. As in sessions 3 and 4, with one bound added. You may change the geometry, the bodies and their collision shapes and contact settings, the joints and their limits, the actuators, and the parameters that set them, through the ordinary edit and accept path, with these bounds:
- it stays a four-legged robot on one free base, and the spec's feet stay the four foot bodies `result["c_foot_fl"]`, `result["c_foot_fr"]`, `result["c_foot_rl"]` and `result["c_foot_rr"]`;
- the floor and its collision plane are not yours to change, and neither is anything that sets the physics for every body (timestep, gravity, solver);
- an actuator stays one the catalog offers, at its catalog limits;
- no contact margin or gap on any shape (`margin_mm` and `gap_mm` stay 0): an evaluation of a model that has one is void.
The spec's thresholds are written in the mechanism's own scale, so three constants follow the mechanism and nothing else: `HIP_MM` and `WEIGHT_N` must equal the accepted model's hip height and weight, which the accepted attempt's task bundle states under `success.scale` (`hip_height_mm`, `weight_n`) and `evaluate` reports under `rig`; and `SPEC_LIFT` keeps its written formula. If a mechanism change moves either value, set the constant to the new value before you train. An evaluation whose `HIP_MM` or `WEIGHT_N` is not its model's is void. A run's reason says what changed and which measurement motivated it. The trainer refuses a warm start across a change to the model or to what the network reads or emits, so after such a change start fresh.

THE RULES FOR TRAINING. Unchanged. Every run is pre-registered by train_start with a wall-clock budget of at most 2400 seconds; for scale, 800 iterations of 2048 environments took about 2,100 to 2,200 s on this machine on the old spring, and the new spring has not been timed. One run at a time. The training seed is never one of 1101-1110. A run's reason cites a measurement from the previous evaluation: the failing predicate, its measured value and the limit. After each run: store the policy, declare it on the task it was trained on, call evaluate, and read the verdict, the per-seed rows, the metrics, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips. A failed evaluation is diagnosed before the next run: which predicate failed, by how much, on which seeds, and what in the task or the mechanism you believe caused it; then say whether the change helped.

WHEN TO STOP. This session is at most three runs. Stop when an evaluation passes on all ten seeds, or when this session has trained and evaluated three runs, whichever comes first. A start the trainer refuses before its first iteration is not a run. Do not stop after a failed evaluation while runs remain. End with a short report: each run of this session, what it changed in the task or the mechanism and why, its evaluation, and what you would change next.
