STYLE: THE ROBOT CREATURE. The project chose this style: a machine that takes the form of an animal or a character -- a raptor, a bird, a cat, a dog, a lizard -- built as a real robot of actuators, frame, panels and sensors, whose likeness comes from its silhouette, its proportions and the way it moves, never from a costume over a machine. Its rules add to the design rules above, and where they say more, they win.

- THE ANATOMY IS THE JOINT LIST. In step 1, write the animal's moving anatomy as a table in DECISIONS.md -- region, joints and their axes, the actuator for each, the range -- before you choose a part. Start from the animal, not from the machine you would build anyway: a long neck has at least two joints, at its root and under the head; the head pitches; a jaw that is part of the animal's character opens; a long tail has at least two segments and moves at its root in yaw and pitch; arms have a shoulder and an elbow; toes, claws or wings the animal is known for move. A region left rigid gets a measured reason in the table and its `reason=` in the script: a mass, a torque or a collision you computed, never "decoration". Declare every region in the script, so the build's anatomy block can show the design is complete.
- SIZE THE ACTUATOR TO THE JOINT. The legs that carry the body take the large actuators; the neck, head, jaw, tail, arms and toes carry far less and take the smallest actuator that holds their load -- a small QDD or a bus servo -- computed from the segment's mass and lever, and written in docs/actuators.md. Mount the heavy ones near the body and drive a distal joint through a pushrod or a cable where the mass at the tip would cost more than the linkage.
- THE ACTUATOR IS THE JOINT, AND IT SHOWS. Each actuated axis is a round housing grown around its drive (`lib.housing`), concentric with the axis, with the drive's face or a ring around it in the accent, so the joint reads as the joint. Never bury a drive inside a panel shaped like a muscle, and never hide a joint under a bellows or a skin. Repeat one housing design across the machine, scaled to each actuator.
- SHELLS SIT ON THE MASSES; LINKS DO THE WORK. The torso, the head and the clusters of drives at the hips and shoulders carry panels. The long members -- the shin and tarsus, the neck and tail segments, the arms -- are slender structural links, tapered to their load, that show their structure. A creature that is all shell is a costume; one with no panels is a test rig.
- PANELS WRAP THE MECHANISM. A panel may curve -- gently bent, form-fitting -- but each one is grown from what it covers (`part.panel(part.envelope(over=[...]), ...)`), at a small clearance and a rolling radius the form wants (a creature's covers often sit at 20-40), thin, split at seams that follow the form and the access you need, open where a drive or a limb leaves and where the sensor looks out, and screwed to the frame that carries it. A panel sized by eye around nothing is the defect: an egg, a dome, a sausage or a cone over the electronics. Declare each `role="panel", covers=[...]`; the build's panel check (`fit.panels`) fails one that floats, collides or is unmounted, and names an egg -- fix what it names before you call the covers done.
- SEGMENTS, NOT A SKIN, WHERE IT BENDS. A neck or a tail that bends is a chain of discrete segments, each its own part on its own joint, overlapping like plates or parted by a visible joint ring, tapering along the chain -- never one loft over its whole length.
- THE CHARACTER IS A REAL SENSOR. The head carries the machine's real forward sensor -- a camera or a range sensor -- held by its own screws, and its bezel, a ring in the accent, is what reads as the eye. One sensor, or a matched pair where the part is a stereo camera; never painted eyes, a visor with dots or a mouth drawn on a panel.
- TWO TONES AND ONE FUNCTIONAL ACCENT. A light shell and a dark mechanism, or the reverse, and one accent on what works: joint rings, the sensor bezel, a power cable, foot pads. The accent is well under a tenth of the surface, never a stripe.
- LIKENESS BY SILHOUETTE AND PROPORTION. Take the proportions from the animal: its leg segment ratios (a bird's or a raptor's digitigrade leg with a long tarsus, a cat's long back and short heel), its neck and tail length against its body, its head size, its posture. Record the proportions and where you took them in DECISIONS.md. Then `{{look}}` at `right`: the side silhouette alone, in black, should name the animal.
- THE BAR IS A REAL PRODUCT. Finish it as a real machine is finished -- consistent radii, housings that read as cast or machined, ordered fasteners, a cable on a deliberate path -- the robot a company would ship, not a model of one.
- WHEN YOU LOOK, ALSO NAME a region of the anatomy table that does not move, a neck or tail that is one loft, a shell that floats over what it covers, a drive buried in a panel, a part that is costume rather than structure, a face, and a side silhouette that does not read as the animal.
