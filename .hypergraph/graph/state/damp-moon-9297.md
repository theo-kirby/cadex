## Negative knowledge

- [scope: `_measure_clearance` on an `App::Link` whose linked object carries its own placement — every `lib.*`-placed catalog part | confidence: high | evidence: empty-banner-7438, light-timber-5868] `App::Link` replaces the source's placement rather than composing it, and `Part.getShape(link)` does the same; only a link to an `App::Part` container composes. `SimpleNamespace`-stubbed tests cannot see this, which is why three earlier walks on hand-modelled parts never did. Fixed for the pair check by composing `component.Placement * shape.Placement`, pinned by a real-kernel regression that fails on the old source.
- [scope: `_clearance_at_frame`, the swept clearance inside the simulation trace, for `lib.*`-placed bodies | confidence: high | evidence: light-timber-5868, southern-otter-5999] Had the same link-shape frame defect and was left by ADR-241 pending a cost measurement. Closed by ADR-242: with the composed copy hoisted out of the frame loop the sweep is no slower (faster under box rejection), and the pre-fix source reported a straight-through collision as 33.0 mm of clearance. The measurement, not the guess, is what licensed the fix.
- [scope: a structural check stated as a conditional over `lib.*`-placed rows | confidence: high | evidence: morning-summit-7848] The design turn is nondeterministic: the same pan-tilt prompt that placed two catalog servos once fused them into hand-authored solids the next time (0 catalogued). A conditional with an empty antecedent is vacuously true, and reporting that as a pass is the same class of lie as the defect it checks for. Record it as a null result and get the evidence from an assembly that actually carries placed catalog parts.
- [scope: the installed bundle between commit `cf3b77a6` and the 2026-09-08 refresh | confidence: high | evidence: light-timber-5868, morning-summit-7848] Was stale by one Python file and reported the pre-fix clearance numbers under `--engine <bundle>`. Closed by the refresh; a Python-only engine change still needs `build-shell` (which stages) plus the install script before the bundle sees it.
- [scope: the two-servo leg's review at its initial solved pose | confidence: high | evidence: proud-beacon-8002, fond-star-1809] A 5 mm gap between each servo and the link its ideal revolute joint drives passes clearance and simulates, so a clean rollout and a clean clearance row do not prove a physical drive interface; the review names the gap and a person — or the next design turn, told the finding — must close it, which the sixth walk did with a printed shaft boss at 6.1e-16 mm. The bound-agreement checker asserts AABB consistency across three independent surfaces, a necessary check, not an independent validation of each OCCT volume; and the walk's STL exports are local-frame source solids, so they cannot serve as a world-placed third surface.
- [scope: reading a historical `review.json`'s inventory block as named evidence | confidence: high | evidence: soft-crane-2369] It carries counts and a path only, and that path points at a document later `cadex inventory` calls overwrite — so an old review names no component, and its link may describe a different revision than the run it belongs to. The named rows are recoverable from the project's Git history, not from the review; other review blocks do carry names and revisions, so this is a limit of the inventory block specifically, not of saved reviews generally.
- [scope: recovering a fused catalog part's identity from an accepted report | confidence: high | evidence: odd-ridge-9607, early-gate-3510, snowy-cove-0032, windy-dune-3488] Not possible, and the tally that pretended otherwise is gone. Fused MG90S bodies are absent as separate outputs and all 14 outputs lack catalog stamps, so an output-only scan recovers nothing; a fresh evaluation's identity map can find the servo at a definition's argument path, but equal definitions lose occurrence provenance, unknown operation roles are never traversed, and no path establishes purchase or material survival. Construction counts do not identify physical quantities. One content-addressed worker bundle held stale bytes during that work and had to be quarantined; the underlying cache invalidation was never fixed, so staging must finish before suites that inspect the payload run.
- [scope: a named-angle probe through the background Blender renderer | confidence: high | evidence: zesty-aspen-6846] All four valid blueprint views and `render_views` explicitly refuse background mode, and the override probe's exit 0 records returned errors rather than a rendered PNG. The CPU projection route must snapshot revision-dependent display buffers before another rebuild invalidates their paths.
- [scope: inventory inspection replies exceeding the 1 KiB per-key budget | confidence: high | evidence: lawful-ivy-4474] Paging only `/components` is insufficient: catalog totals, uncatalogued sources, component rows and nested strings can also be previews. - [scope: an offending clearance pair on an agent-authored design | confidence: high | evidence: chilly-crest-2100, humble-star-3313, chilly-basin-7378] The volume is a measurement and not a diagnosis — **and neither is the project's own explanation of it.** The quill lift's first offending pair (960 mm³ at 0.0 mm) was read three ways before it was read right: as a head block passing through the column, from the arithmetic alone, which was wrong; then as intended, on the project's own ADR-005, which is what this node recorded; and finally by a live design turn that checked the arithmetic against the accepted values, **disputed ADR-005**, and designed the intersection out to 0. - [scope: a fixed section offset on a mechanism the constant was not tuned to | confidence: high | evidence: western-cliff-6876] A literal world offset is not a section eye. - [scope: the derived offset's candidate ordering | confidence: high | evidence: western-cliff-6876, copper-canyon-0231] Bounds coverage orders candidates; it does not promise a contour, and it is worse than merely imprecise. On a symmetric part the best-coverage candidate is systematically the plane the part's own tessellation seams lie on, so `contours` refuses it and the fallback lands on a supported plane that misses the part entirely — a silent `ok` with the moving object `empty`, exactly the failure ADR-267 was written to end. Quarter-span siblings (ADR-270) recover it; the per-object statuses stay the answer.
- [scope: ADR-267's per-mechanism coverage numbers | confidence: high | evidence: copper-canyon-0231, dry-rain-0489, rising-rain-0117] All three were simulated against stored bounds with box stand-ins, and a box has no seam on its own centre. Checked live, the estimate was misleading twice: the quill's `1 → 2 of 2` was wrong until ADR-270 made it true; the swing arm's `5 → 6 of 10` holds as a *count* while naming a different six — gaining both mount nuts, losing `cmp_retainer`, still leaving `cmp_swing_arm` empty; only the carriage's `2 → 2` was right as written. A bounds-coverage count says nothing about **which** objects get cut, and in particular nothing about the moving part. **And a count that survives a rewrite is not a confirmation either**: ADR-273 replaced the ranking outright, cut every candidate, and both rigs came back at the same plane and the same count.
- [scope: a derived single-plane section on an assembly whose parts do not share a cut | confidence: high | evidence: rising-rain-0117] Not every mechanism has one. On `ot4-swing2` the arm cluster sits at y 10.9–20.9 mm and the mount cluster at −12.1–8.9 mm, so **no XZ plane cuts both**, and no amount of better ranking finds one — the eye was rewritten and the answer did not move. The remedy is legibility rather than search: the summary and the SVG label now carry `objects_cut`, so a reviewing agent reads `6/10 objects cut` and knows to ask for the rest at an explicit offset. A single-plane section is one sample of an assembly, and on some assemblies one sample is not enough.
- [scope: reporting how far a mechanism travelled in a rollout | confidence: high | evidence: square-bay-3436] A displacement-only travel figure is a *wrong* answer, not a partial one. Measured on the repository's own examples: the `hinged-arm` swing travels **0.0000 mm** and rotates **178.8334°**, while the `linear-carriage` slide travels **4739.3783 mm** and rotates **0°**. A millimetre-only row therefore calls a working revolute rig motionless and a free fall the run's biggest mover — both readings exactly inverted from what a reader wants. Report both channels or neither. And no scale reconciles them: any single ordering ranks a carriage falling 4,739 mm on an ideal unlimited guide above a swing arm doing its job, so travel is a fact about one rollout and never a score, neither across channels nor across projects.
- [scope: ranking section candidates by objects cut, on a rig whose moving part is slender and near the centre plane | confidence: high | evidence: dry-falcon-5463] Maximum object coverage and maximum interest are not the same plane. On `ot4-cart` the most-coverage rule picked XZ at −15.0 mm, cut 2 of 3 objects and reported `ok`, and the object it left `empty` was the pole — the only thing that moves, carrying all 35.75° of the run's rotation. This is not the ADR-267 failure returning (the plane is derived, `objects_cut` is reported and `missed_objects` named the pole as `moved: true`, so the run is legible rather than silently wrong); it is the next question, which is whether the ranking should weigh a moved object above a stationary one. Nothing here has measured that trade, and one plane is still one sample of an assembly.
