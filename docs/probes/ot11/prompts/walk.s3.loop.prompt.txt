This project is the copy of ot10's accepted quadruped that you have trained seven times, over two sessions, to walk with real steps at a commanded speed. This is a third session of the same loop. The behaviour, the frozen success spec, the evaluation seeds and the rules for training are unchanged. The spec is in the script as `walk_spec`, passed to the task as `success=walk_spec` with `goals=[command]`, and you must never loosen, drop or reword a predicate, a seed, a condition or the goal.

WHAT IS NEW IN THIS SESSION: YOU MAY CHANGE THE MECHANISM. In session 2 you diagnosed W10 as largely a property of the robot, not of the reward ("a stiffer contact or a different foot is a mechanism change I was not allowed to make"). That limit is lifted. You may now change the geometry, the bodies and their collision shapes and contact settings, the joints and their limits, the actuators, and the parameters that set them, through the ordinary edit and accept path, with these bounds:
- it stays a four-legged robot on one free base, and the spec's feet stay the four foot bodies `result["c_foot_fl"]`, `result["c_foot_fr"]`, `result["c_foot_rl"]` and `result["c_foot_rr"]`;
- the floor and its collision plane are not yours to change, and neither is anything that sets the physics for every body (timestep, gravity, solver);
- an actuator stays one the catalog offers, at its catalog limits.
The spec's thresholds are written in the mechanism's own scale, so three constants follow the mechanism and nothing else: `HIP_MM` and `WEIGHT_N` must equal the accepted model's hip height and weight, which the accepted attempt's task bundle states under `success.scale` (`hip_height_mm`, `weight_n`) and `evaluate` reports under `rig`; and `SPEC_LIFT` keeps its written formula. If a mechanism change moves either value, set the constant to the new value before you train. An evaluation whose `HIP_MM` or `WEIGHT_N` is not its model's is void. A mechanism change is a change like any other: a run's reason says what changed and which measurement motivated it. A policy trained on one mechanism may not suit another, so say in the reason why a warm start still makes sense, or start fresh.

WHERE THE LOOP STANDS. Call train_status with no run first and read every run and evaluation. All seven runs failed all ten seeds. Rounds 5 to 7 walked forward at the commanded speed, so W3 is no longer the problem. What no round has moved: W8-low (the foot least often in stance is on the ground 0.16 to 0.29 of the time; the limit is 0.40) and W10 (the deepest foot after the settle sits 0.09 to 0.21 hip heights in the floor; the limit is -0.05). W9 has not met 1.5 in any round: one or two feet step and the others are dragged, and which feet moved with each reward revision. r7-relswing collapsed because its policy scored about -2.1 per step under the new reward from its first iteration. Read the evaluations themselves (the rows, the metrics, the per-foot numbers, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips) before deciding anything; these sentences are a summary, not the evidence.

THE RULES FOR TRAINING. Unchanged. Every run is pre-registered by train_start with a wall-clock budget of at most 2400 seconds; for scale, 1000 iterations of 2048 environments took about 1900 s on this machine. One run at a time. The training seed is never one of 1101-1110. A run's reason cites a measurement from the previous evaluation: the failing predicate, its measured value and the limit. After each run: store the policy, declare it on the task it was trained on, call evaluate, and read the verdict, the per-seed rows, the metrics, the reward terms, the endings and the filmstrips. A failed evaluation is diagnosed before the next run: which predicate failed, by how much, on which seeds, and what in the task or the mechanism you believe caused it; then say whether the change helped.

WHEN TO STOP. This session is at most three runs. Stop when an evaluation passes on all ten seeds, or when ten runs have been trained and evaluated in total on this project (seven from the first two sessions and three from this one), whichever comes first. Do not stop after a failed evaluation while runs remain. End with a short report: each run of this session, what it changed in the task or the mechanism and why, its evaluation, and what you would change next.
